# Licensing Deal Patterns

The structural building blocks of IP commercialization deals. Don't draft
from scratch — pick the right pattern, adapt it.

## L1 — Deal type taxonomy

### L1.1 Exclusive license
Only the licensee may use the IP within the granted scope.

- **Use when:** licensor wants to monetise without operating the market;
  licensee needs exclusivity to justify investment in commercialization
- **Watch:** anti-trust if granting too broadly + too long; ensure narrow
  field/territory carve-outs to allow future deals
- **Compensation:** highest of all license types — usually higher upfront +
  higher running royalty; sometimes equity component

### L1.2 Sole license
Licensee is exclusive vs other licensees, but licensor retains right to use.

- **Use when:** licensor still wants to operate the market itself;
  licensee accepts non-blocking licensor presence
- **Watch:** licensee may push back hard on the licensor-retains clause;
  define scope of licensor's reserved use carefully
- **Compensation:** between exclusive and non-exclusive

### L1.3 Non-exclusive license
Multiple licensees may operate in the same scope.

- **Use when:** licensor wants broad adoption (e.g., standards-essential
  patents, open-platform plays); the IP is more valuable widely deployed
- **Watch:** anti-stacking with later licensees; most-favored-nation pressure
  from early licensees
- **Compensation:** lower upfront, lower running royalty, but volume across
  many licensees compensates

### L1.4 Field-of-use license
Restricts use to specific industries, applications, or product categories.

- **Use when:** asset has multiple commercial applications and you want to
  monetise each separately
- **Example:** license patent X for use in healthcare diagnostic devices, but
  retain rights for industrial inspection use
- **Watch:** field definitions are where deals fight 5 years later. Be
  explicit. "Healthcare" alone is too broad. "In vitro diagnostic devices
  approved by FDA Class II or higher" is more defensible.

### L1.5 Territory license
Restricts use to specific countries or regions.

- **Use when:** market structure varies by region (different licensees,
  regulatory regimes, language)
- **Watch:** "Asia-Pacific" must be a list of specific countries. "All
  markets except those listed" creates ambiguity. List both ways.
- **Watch:** parallel imports (licensed product in one territory makes its
  way to another) — address explicitly

### L1.6 Sublicense rights
Licensee may grant sub-licenses to third parties.

- **Use when:** licensee operates an ecosystem (platform plays); licensee
  is downstream integrator that will partner with others
- **Watch:** licensor share of sublicense revenue; usually 25-50% of
  sublicense royalty flows back to original licensor
- **Watch:** quality control over sub-licensees (especially for trademarks)

### L1.7 Cross-license
Two parties license to each other.

- **Use when:** both parties have IP the other needs; settles infringement
  disputes; standard in semiconductors and telecoms
- **Watch:** balancing payment if one side's portfolio is larger;
  freedom-to-operate on each side's products
- **Compensation:** often net-zero or net-payment from larger-portfolio side

### L1.8 Outright sale (assignment)
Transfer of ownership, not a license.

- **Use when:** licensor doesn't want ongoing royalty admin; licensee wants
  full control including ability to enforce
- **Watch:** retain back-license for licensor's operating use if needed
- **Compensation:** lump sum, typically ~7-15× expected 1-year licensing
  revenue

## L2 — Royalty base anatomy

The single highest-leverage clause in any term sheet. "Net sales" is not a
definition — it's a placeholder for a definition.

### L2.1 Gross sales
- All revenue from licensed product, no deductions
- **Pros:** simple, audit-friendly
- **Cons:** licensee resists strongly; doesn't match commercial reality

### L2.2 Net sales (industry-standard, but define carefully)
- Gross minus: returns, discounts, taxes, shipping, intercompany sales
- **MUST define:** what discounts (volume? rebates? promotional?), what taxes
  (sales tax only? VAT? withholding tax?), what intercompany rules (transfer
  prices?)
- **Sample clean definition:** "Net Sales means gross invoiced amounts for
  Licensed Products sold by Licensee to non-affiliated third parties, less
  (a) trade and quantity discounts actually allowed, (b) refunds for returned
  Licensed Products, (c) taxes legally imposed on the sale and separately
  invoiced, (d) outbound shipping and insurance separately invoiced. No
  deduction for marketing expenses, sales commissions, or bad debt."

### L2.3 Net revenue (less common, more aggressive deductions)
- Net sales minus cost of goods, marketing, distribution
- **Use when:** licensee insists on profit-based royalty; only when comparables
  support
- **Watch:** opens up cost accounting disputes; harder to audit

### L2.4 Per-unit royalty (alternative to %)
- Fixed dollar amount per unit shipped
- **Use when:** unit economics are stable; product price is volatile; common
  in consumer products
- **Watch:** doesn't capture price increases; consider escalator clause

### L2.5 Tiered royalty
- Royalty rate varies with cumulative volume or revenue
- **Use when:** licensor wants to share upside above thresholds; licensee
  wants relief at high volumes
- **Watch:** define tier boundaries unambiguously; specify reset frequency
  (annual / per-product / over license term)

## L3 — Compensation building blocks

### L3.1 Upfront license fee
- Paid at signing, non-refundable
- Anchors the deal value; signals seriousness
- Typical range: 5-25% of expected total deal value
- Use when: licensor needs immediate value; protects against licensee abandonment

### L3.2 Running royalty
- % of net sales (per L2 definition) paid quarterly or annually
- Typical structure: see `ip-valuation-method` §V2 industry benchmarks
- **Watch:** payment timing (60/90/120 days from quarter end), late-payment interest

### L3.3 Milestone payments
- Lump sums triggered by specific events
- **Common milestones:** regulatory approval, first commercial sale, cumulative
  revenue threshold ($1M, $10M, $50M), expansion to new territory
- **Watch:** milestone language must be unambiguous. "First commercial sale"
  is ambiguous (test sale? bona-fide third-party sale?). Define exactly.

### L3.4 Minimum guaranteed royalties (MGR)
- Floor on royalty payments regardless of actual sales
- **Use when:** licensor needs revenue certainty; licensee has agreed to
  exclusive/sole license (justifies forcing them to perform)
- **Typical structure:** $X/year escalating after year 2 or 3
- **Watch:** non-payment of MGR should be explicit termination event;
  otherwise licensee can sit on exclusive license without using it

### L3.5 Maximum royalty cap
- Ceiling on royalty payments
- **Use when:** licensee has unusually large sales potential and pushes back
  on uncapped rate; rare and high-leverage
- **Watch:** caps are licensor-unfriendly; only grant in exchange for higher
  upfront or other concessions

### L3.6 Equity component
- Licensor takes equity in licensee company (or NewCo)
- **Use when:** licensee is early-stage and short on cash; licensor wants
  upside if commercialization succeeds
- **Watch:** equity is illiquid; valuation at issuance; anti-dilution

### L3.7 Sublicense revenue share
- % of sub-license royalties flowing back to original licensor
- Typical: 25-50% of sublicense royalty (NOT of sub-licensee's net sales)
- **Watch:** this is independent of running royalty on direct sales; needs
  separate clause

## L4 — Critical clauses every term sheet must address

These are the ones that, if missed, kill the deal at definitive-agreement
stage or 3 years into the relationship.

### L4.1 Improvements / derivative IP
- Who owns improvements made by licensee?
- Three options: licensor / licensee / shared
- **Default recommendation:** licensee owns improvements they make solely;
  shared ownership for joint improvements; licensor gets non-exclusive
  back-license for licensee-made improvements

### L4.2 Reps and warranties
- What licensor represents about the IP (ownership, non-infringement of
  third-party IP, validity)
- **Watch:** "non-infringement" rep is the riskiest; many licensors give it
  only to their actual knowledge ("to Licensor's knowledge, the IP does not
  infringe...")

### L4.3 Indemnification
- Who covers damages if a third party sues over the IP?
- Bilateral indemnity is the norm: licensor indemnifies for IP-validity
  claims; licensee indemnifies for product-liability claims arising from
  their products

### L4.4 Audit rights
- Licensor's right to audit licensee's royalty calculations
- Typical: 1× per year, with 30 days notice, paid by licensor unless
  audit finds >5% under-payment (then licensee pays)

### L4.5 Termination
- Define every termination event:
  - Material breach (with cure period)
  - Bankruptcy / insolvency
  - Failure to meet MGR
  - Failure to commercialize within X months
  - Change of control of licensee (often optional)
- Define what happens on termination: license rights revert? licensee
  inventory may be sold for X months? licensor gets right to acquire
  improvements?

### L4.6 Most-favored-nation (avoid unless leveraged)
- "If licensor grants better terms to anyone else, licensee gets those terms"
- **Recommendation:** REJECT MFN unless asymmetric leverage justifies. MFN
  destroys ability to vary terms by licensee profile. If granted, narrow
  scope to specific commercial terms (royalty rate only, not entire deal).

### L4.7 Anti-assignment
- Whether licensee may transfer the license (e.g., on acquisition)
- **Default:** licensor consent required, not unreasonably withheld; automatic
  assignment to acquirer in change-of-control transactions sometimes allowed

### L4.8 Patent maintenance and enforcement
- Who pays maintenance fees on licensed patents?
- Who decides whether to enforce against infringers?
- **Default for exclusive licenses:** licensee pays maintenance; licensor
  controls enforcement with consultation; licensee may enforce if licensor
  declines

### L4.9 Confidentiality
- Treatment of trade secrets / confidential know-how shared as part of license
- Survives termination (trade secrets) vs limited duration (general confidential
  business info)

### L4.10 Governing law and dispute resolution
- Which jurisdiction's law applies; arbitration vs litigation; venue
- **Default for AU / NZ deals:** New South Wales / Victoria, arbitration
  under ACICA rules for international licensees

## L5 — Deal-killer clauses to flag explicitly

When you see these in a draft (yours or counterparty's), surface them in
review. They're not necessarily wrong, but they require explicit human decision.

- L5.1 Most-favored-nation (per L4.6)
- L5.2 Anti-assignment without consent (blocks licensor's exit)
- L5.3 No-challenge clause (licensee agrees not to challenge patent validity)
- L5.4 Exclusivity with no minimum performance obligation (licensee can sit on it)
- L5.5 Royalty payable beyond patent expiration (potentially unenforceable)
- L5.6 Cross-license to "all current and future IP" (over-broad)
- L5.7 Equity grants without anti-dilution

## L6 — Term sheet vs definitive agreement

Term sheets are commercial documents, 5-15 pages. Definitive licensing
agreements are 30-60 pages with all the boilerplate.

- **What goes in the term sheet:** all of L1-L5 above, in business language
- **What goes in the definitive agreement:** everything in term sheet plus
  detailed warranty schedules, indemnity caps and floors, full royalty
  reporting requirements, definitions appendix, governing-law clauses,
  notice provisions, force majeure, etc.
- **Practical rule:** if it's a number or a structural decision, it's in the
  term sheet. If it's procedural / boilerplate, it's in the definitive.

The term sheet drafter (this agent) does NOT draft the definitive agreement.
That's a licensed lawyer's work. State this explicitly in every draft.
